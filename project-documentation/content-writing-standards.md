# Purpose

Define how content for `Your House in Cash` should be written, which content decisions Codex may make autonomously, and which require explicit user approval. These standards cover legal, financial, real-estate, informational, and commercial content decisions.

# 1. External Referral & Next-Step Rule

When content recommends that a homeowner contact, hire, use, or rely on an external professional, service, organization, or resource as a next step, explicit user approval is required before that recommendation may be added to the content.

Examples include:

- Real Estate Agent / Realtor
- Probate Attorney
- CPA / Tax Professional
- Appraiser
- Contractor
- Property Manager
- Title Company
- Government office or external service

Do not autonomously assume that referring the homeowner to an external party is the preferred next step.

`Your House in Cash` may have another way to provide the information, assist the homeowner, or present an alternative path that we prefer to offer before directing the homeowner elsewhere.

It is permitted to explain **factually what an external professional or organization does** when that information is necessary to understand the subject.

Approval is required when the content moves from explanation to an **actionable recommendation** that the homeowner contact, hire, use, consult, or otherwise rely on that external party as a next step.

If such a decision arises during content development, Codex must:

1. Identify the unresolved referral / next-step decision.
2. Do not insert the recommendation autonomously.
3. Briefly present the reasonable available options.
4. Ask for user approval before adding the recommendation to the content.

# 2. Natural Service Integration Rule

When a homeowner reaches a point in the content where `Your House in Cash` can genuinely help them accomplish the next logical step, the company should be introduced naturally as a relevant option.

Do not force the company into the content merely to create a conversion opportunity.

The service should appear only when it directly helps solve the homeowner’s current problem, answer an immediate need, or move them toward the next decision.

Examples may include:

- Understanding what the property may be worth in an as-is direct sale.
- Receiving a no-obligation cash offer for comparison.
- Selling without making repairs.
- Selling a property with unwanted belongings still inside.
- Avoiding repeated showings or a traditional listing process.
- Choosing a flexible closing timeline.

If `Your House in Cash` is not genuinely relevant to the homeowner’s current need, do not insert a CTA or recommendation.

When the service is relevant, describe accurately what it provides. Do not present:

- A cash offer as an appraisal.
- A direct-sale offer as equivalent to market value.
- The company as providing legal, tax, or other professional services it does not provide.

A useful test is:

**If Your House in Cash were removed from this point in the content, would the homeowner lose a genuinely relevant way to accomplish what they are currently trying to do?**

- If yes, the service may be integrated naturally.
- If no, do not insert it merely for conversion.

This rule should work together with Rule 1:

- Do not autonomously send the homeowner to an external provider without approval.
- Do naturally introduce `Your House in Cash` when the company itself is a legitimate and relevant solution to the homeowner’s current need.
