# Cash Offer vs. Listing With an Agent in Ohio: Which Leaves You With More Money?

Selling on the open market often brings a higher price than taking a direct cash offer. When you list a house, more people have a chance to see it, including buyers looking for a place to live. If two or three buyers want the same house, they may be willing to offer more to get it. ([Zillow Research][2])

Another reason homes sell for more on the open market is that most sellers do some work to fix them up before selling. Buyers are often willing to pay more for a house they can move into without making repairs first. ([Zillow’s 2026 seller survey][3])

How much you keep depends on the house, the offers you receive, and what it costs to get to closing. If you spend money fixing up the house, pay agent fees and closing costs, or carry the property for several extra months, a higher sale price can still leave you with less than a direct cash offer.

## Cash Offer vs. Listing a Rental in Euclid, Ohio

Let’s look at a real example from [Euclid, Ohio](/sell-your-house-fast-as-is-euclid). The tenant had moved out, and the owner decided to sell the vacant rental. The owner figured it could use some touch-ups. The house went on the market as-is for **$125,000**, and a cash buyer offered **$92,500**.

If the house sells at asking price, listing starts with a price advantage. Some of that difference would go toward touch-ups, agent fees, closing costs, and the bills that keep coming while the house sits empty.

Here’s what each option could leave the owner with before any mortgage or lien payoff.

| Item | Direct cash offer | Listing sells at asking price | What it covers |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Sale price | $92,500 | $125,000 | The cash buyer’s offer and the seller’s asking price. |
| Touch-ups | $0 | $5,000 to $10,000 | Work after the tenant moves out, such as painting and minor repairs. |
| Agent fees and seller closing costs | $0 | $9,500 | Agent fees and the seller’s share of title, settlement, and transfer charges. |
| Carrying costs | $0 | $1,600 to $2,400 for 60 to 90 days | Insurance, property taxes, utilities, and basic upkeep while the house sits empty. |
| **Estimated amount after these costs** | **$92,500** | **$103,100 to $108,900** | Sale price minus the costs above, before any mortgage or lien payoff. |

In this example, the higher sale price would cover the touch-ups, fees, and cost of keeping the house empty and still leave the owner with more than the cash offer. That depends on getting the asking price and closing on schedule. If the owner has to drop the price or the buyer’s financing falls through and the house sits empty longer, that advantage can shrink quickly.

## What if you have to reduce the price or wait longer?

Suppose the best offer comes in at **$115,000**. Using **$7,500 in touch-ups**, **$9,500 in agent fees and closing costs**, and **$2,400 in carrying costs for 90 days**, listing would leave **$95,600**, just **$3,100 more** than the cash offer.

Now suppose the buyer can’t get final mortgage approval and the deal falls through. Depending on the purchase agreement, a financing contingency can allow the buyer to cancel. The seller may have to put the house back on the market while still paying for insurance, property taxes, utilities, and upkeep. One extra month at $800 would reduce the listing’s advantage to **$2,300**. If the next buyer also negotiates a **$3,500 closing credit**, the estimated amount falls to **$91,300**, or **$1,200 less** than the cash offer.

## Which option makes sense for you?

If you’re ready to sell, compare what you’d expect to keep from listing with what you’d keep from a direct cash offer. Then think about how much time and work you want to put into the sale. If you’re still open to finding another tenant, that’s worth weighing too. [Should you keep your rental or sell it?](/landlords)

You might come out ahead on the open market, but a lot can change between listing and closing. Buyers may offer less than you’re asking, request a credit after the inspection, or lose their financing. If a deal falls through, you may be back to scheduling showings and paying the bills on an empty house while you wait for another buyer.
