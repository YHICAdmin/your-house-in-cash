# Garfield Heights Point-of-Sale Inspection: What Sellers Need to Know

If you are selling a property in Garfield Heights, a point-of-sale inspection—usually called a POS—is part of the sale process. It is not optional: the City requires a current Certificate of Inspection or Certificate of Compliance before an owner sells, transfers, conveys an interest in, or enters an agreement to sell a covered property.[1]

That requirement applies to homes, commercial buildings, and other property with buildings. A POS does not automatically mean you must repair everything before closing, but it does mean the inspection and the City’s transfer requirements need to be addressed before title can transfer.

## What is a POS?

A POS is a municipal inspection connected to the sale or transfer of a property. In Garfield Heights, the City describes it as an exterior inspection and requires a Compliance Certificate for transfer of title.[2]

The inspection is different from a buyer’s private home inspection. Its purpose is to identify violations of the City’s applicable codes at the time of inspection. The Certificate of Inspection identifies the property and owner, states its authorized use and occupancy, and lists the violations found, if any.[3]

## A POS is mandatory when you sell a house in Garfield Heights

For a normal sale, this is a required step—not something a seller can skip because the buyer is purchasing as is. The City code requires the owner to obtain a Certificate of Inspection before selling or entering into an agreement to sell, and the prospective buyer must receive a copy of a current Certificate of Inspection or Certificate of Compliance issued within the prior year.[1]

The City’s code does not apply to an individual transfer by inheritance or gift when no bona fide sale is intended.[1] Court-ordered sales have their own timing rule: if there is no current Certificate of Compliance, the purchaser must apply for an inspection within 30 days after the sale.[1]

## What does a Garfield Heights POS include?

The City’s inspection looks for code violations connected with the property. The completed Certificate of Inspection records:

- The property address, parcel number, or other identifier
- The owner’s name, address, and telephone number
- The structure’s authorized use and occupancy
- Any City code violations that existed when the inspection took place[3]

In practical terms, the report gives the seller and buyer a written record of the property’s municipal compliance status. The City provides a POS application, a buyer acknowledgement form, an affidavit for a buyer assuming outstanding violations, and a separate consent form for an interior POS inspection.[2]

## How the process works

1. The owner or an authorized agent submits the City’s application for a Certificate of Inspection and pays the required fee.[3]
2. The inspection is scheduled during Housing Inspection Department business hours. The code says the City will cause the inspection to be made within 10 days after receiving the application and fee, subject to its stated exceptions.[3]
3. After access to the property, the City issues the Certificate of Inspection within 10 working days under the code’s schedule.[3]
4. The seller gives the prospective buyer the certificate. The buyer acknowledgement must be completed, notarized, and returned to the City as part of the title-transfer requirements.[2]
5. If violations remain, the City’s POS page says a buyer who assumes them must also complete a notarized affidavit. The buyer then has 90 days from transfer to complete the repairs.[2]

## Typical POS costs in Garfield Heights

The City’s current code sets the municipal inspection fee at:

| Property type | Certificate of Inspection fee |
| --- | --- |
| Dwelling structure | $200 |
| Commercial building structure | $300 |

There is no charge for the first two reinspections. After that, the City charges $35 for each reinspection. Missing a scheduled inspection without at least one full business day’s cancellation notice carries a $25 fee.[4]

The inspection fee is only one part of the possible cost. If the report lists violations, the repair cost depends on what was cited and whether the work is completed before transfer or addressed through the City’s process for outstanding violations. That amount cannot be estimated from the POS fee alone.

## Plan for the POS before your closing date

A Garfield Heights POS is a required part of selling, but it is easier to manage when it is treated as an early sale task rather than a last-minute closing issue. The certificate tells everyone involved what the City found and what must be addressed for the transfer to move forward.
