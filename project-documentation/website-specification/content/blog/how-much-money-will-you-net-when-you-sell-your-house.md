# How Much Money Will You Net When You Sell Your House?

The price on the contract is not the amount you take home. Your net proceeds are what remain after the sale price pays off what you owe and covers the costs assigned to you at closing.

The basic idea is simple:

**Sale price - mortgage payoff - other required payoffs - seller costs = estimated net proceeds**

Before you agree to a sale, ask for an estimate that shows each deduction. It gives you a clearer picture than looking at the offer price alone.

## Costs that can reduce your net proceeds

Every sale is different, but these are common deductions from a seller's proceeds.

### Mortgage payoff

If you still have a mortgage or home-equity loan, it is usually paid from the sale proceeds at closing. The payoff amount can be different from the balance on your monthly statement because it may include interest through the payoff date and other charges.

### Liens, unpaid taxes, and other claims

Property taxes, association balances, judgment liens, or other recorded claims may need to be paid or resolved before ownership can transfer. These are not optional closing costs; they are amounts tied to the property or sale that can reduce what you receive.

### Agent commissions and listing expenses

In a traditional listing, the seller may agree to pay a real-estate commission and may also pay for photography, staging, repairs, or other preparation. The amount and who pays it depend on the listing agreement and purchase contract.

### Closing and transfer costs

Closing costs can include title or settlement fees, recording fees, transfer taxes or conveyance charges, and prorated taxes or association charges. Which party pays each cost depends on the contract, local practice, and applicable law. The [Consumer Financial Protection Bureau explains](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-fees-or-charges-are-paid-when-closing-on-a-mortgage-and-who-pays-them-en-1845/) that the buyer generally pays transaction costs, but a contract or state law can assign some costs to the seller.

### Repairs, credits, and concessions

A buyer may ask for repairs after an inspection or request a credit instead. A seller may agree, decline, offer less, or change another part of the deal. These are common negotiation points, and they directly affect your net proceeds.

## Everything Is Negotiable!

The price is negotiable, but it is not the only number that matters. Buyers and sellers can also negotiate repairs, credits, closing-cost contributions, and which costs each party will pay. [Freddie Mac notes](https://myhome.freddiemac.com/blog/homeownership/20160512-responding-to-counteroffer) that repairs and closing costs are among the terms commonly negotiated in a home sale.

Not every amount can be negotiated away. Your mortgage payoff, an unpaid tax bill, or a recorded lien still has to be addressed. The useful question is not just, “What is the offer?” It is, “What will I receive after every required deduction and agreed cost?”

## How an off-market sale can change the costs

Because it is not a traditional on-market sale, you avoid a listing commission. A traditional commission is often around 5% to 6% of the sale price, although the amount depends on the agreement.

In many off-market deals, the buyer takes the house as-is. That means the seller does not need to make repairs before selling. The buyer may also agree to cover some or all of the closing costs, depending on the offer.

In some cases, a direct buyer can help resolve liens as part of the sale. The lien still has to be addressed, but it may not prevent a sale from moving forward.

## Get a clear number before you decide

Ask for a written estimate of net proceeds before accepting an offer. It should show the sale price, every payoff, each agreed cost, and the estimated amount left for you.

That makes it easier to compare offers. A higher price is not always the better offer if it also requires more repairs, credits, commissions, or closing costs. The best comparison is the amount you expect to receive after the deal closes.
